**Why projections matter**
suppose 

slope ~ $\frac{\text{vertical change}}{\text{horizontal distance}}$

elevation change = 100m
adjacent cells are 1000m apart -- different from 100m apart
∆x = 0.01°
∴ 0.01 ≠ physical distance

**Projection Distortion**
1. **Shape**
A region's geometry may look stretched.
2. **Area**
Two equally sized physical regions may appear different sizes.
3. **Distance**
Distances measured on the map may not equal true surface distances.
4. **Direction**
Directions/angles may become distorted.

**Global vs Local thinking**

displaying all of mars -- can tolerate large significant local distortion
- visualisation / overview

routing rover over region of 50km
- care more locally about: distance, slope, geometry
- *local projected coordinate system*

**Mathematical Model**

simple equirectangular projection can conceptually use:
$$ x = R(\lambda-\lambda_0)\cos(\phi_1) $$
$$ y = R(\phi-\phi_0) $$

where:
```
R   = assumed planetary radius
λ   = longitude
φ   = latitude

λ₀  = central/reference longitude
φ₀  = reference latitude
φ₁  = standard latitude
```

```
+
projection parameters
+
lat/lon
        ↓
(x, y)
```
